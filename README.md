# Step 8 — Title and Abstract Screening

[← Previous Step: Download and Manage the Search Results](../step-7-download-and-manage-search-results/)

---

## 🧭 Maya Begins Title and Abstract Screening

Maya has finished downloading and organizing her search results.

She has:

* Saved the original search-result files
* Documented each search query
* Recorded the number of results
* Uploaded the records into her review software
* Identified and removed confirmed duplicates
* Recorded the number of records remaining

Now she is ready for the next stage.

> **Maya:** “My records are clean now. Can I start reading the titles and abstracts?”

> **Mentor:** “Yes. This is the **title and abstract screening** stage.”

> **Maya:** “So I just read each title and abstract and decide whether the study should stay?”

> **Mentor:** “That's the basic idea. But you will encounter some situations that require careful decisions.”

Maya opens her screening tool.

For this project, her mentor recommends **Rayyan** for managing the title and abstract screening process.

---

# 1. Import the Records Into Rayyan

Maya imports her cleaned records into **Rayyan**.

Rayyan allows her to organize and screen records using their:

* Titles
* Abstracts
* Keywords and metadata
* Inclusion and exclusion decisions
* Screening labels
* Notes
* Reviewer decisions

Her workflow now looks like this:

```text
Database Searches
       ↓
Download Search Results
       ↓
Manage Records
       ↓
Identify Duplicates
       ↓
Remove Confirmed Duplicates
       ↓
Import Clean Records Into Rayyan
       ↓
Title and Abstract Screening
```

> **Mentor:** “At this stage, you're not deciding whether a study should be included in the final meta-analysis. You're deciding whether the record appears potentially eligible based on the information currently available.”

---

# 2. Start With the Eligibility Criteria

Before Maya begins screening, she reviews the **eligibility criteria** from her protocol.

For example, Maya may have defined criteria related to:

* Population
* Intervention
* Comparison
* Outcomes
* Study design
* Publication characteristics

> **Mentor:** “Use the criteria you established in your protocol. Don't create new eligibility rules simply because you encounter an unusual record.”

Maya keeps her criteria available while screening.

---

# 3. Screen the Title and Abstract

Maya examines each record.

For each record, she asks:

1. Does the population appear to match?
2. Does the intervention appear to match?
3. Does the study appear to address the relevant outcome?
4. Does the study appear to meet the required study design?
5. Is there enough information to make a decision?

If the record clearly meets the exclusion criteria, Maya excludes it.

If it appears potentially eligible, she keeps it for **full-text screening**.

If she cannot determine eligibility from the title and abstract, she does not automatically exclude it.

> **Mentor:** “When you're unsure, it is usually safer at this stage to keep the record for full-text screening rather than exclude a potentially relevant study.”

---

# 4. What If the Title or Abstract Is in Another Language?

Maya encounters a record written in a language she does not understand.

> **Maya:** “I found a study that looks relevant, but the title and abstract are in another language. What should I do?”

> **Mentor:** “First, check your protocol. Did you specify a language eligibility criterion?”

There are two different situations.

### If the protocol excludes certain languages

Maya applies the predefined eligibility criterion.

She documents the reason for exclusion.

### If the protocol does not exclude the language

Maya should not automatically exclude the study simply because she cannot read the language.

Instead, she should determine whether the record can be assessed using an appropriate translation or language-support process.

For example:

```text
Record in another language
        ↓
Check protocol
        ↓
Language exclusion specified?
      ↙       ↘
    Yes        No
     ↓          ↓
Apply       Consider translation/
criterion   language support
                ↓
          Make screening decision
```

> **Mentor:** “The important point is that language decisions should follow your predefined eligibility criteria rather than being made simply because a record is difficult to read.”

---

# 5. What If There Is a Title but No Abstract?

Maya opens another record.

It has a title, but there is no abstract.

> **Maya:** “This record has a title, but there is no abstract. Should I exclude it?”

> **Mentor:** “Not automatically.”

The absence of an abstract does **not necessarily mean that the study is ineligible**.

Maya first examines the information that is available.

She may find enough information in the title or record metadata to determine that the study clearly meets an exclusion criterion.

If she cannot determine eligibility, she keeps the record for **full-text screening**.

```text
Title available
Abstract missing
       ↓
Can eligibility be determined?
      ↙       ↘
    Yes        No
     ↓          ↓
Make        Keep for
decision    full-text screening
```

> **Mentor:** “Remember: a missing abstract is a missing piece of information—not automatically an exclusion reason.”

---

# 6. What If Two Titles Look Exactly the Same?

Maya notices something strange.

Two records have what appears to be the same title.

She thinks:

> **Maya:** “Didn't we already remove duplicates? Why are these two records still here?”

Her mentor explains that duplicate detection is not always perfect.

Two records may represent the same publication but have differences in how their metadata were entered.

For example:

**Record 1**

```text
The Effect of Artificial Intelligence on Student Learning
```

**Record 2**

```text
THE EFFECT OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE ON STUDENT LEARNING
```

The titles look different because of **capitalization**, but the words are otherwise identical.

A duplicate-detection system may not always identify these records as duplicates, depending on the available metadata and matching process.

> **Mentor:** “That's why you should remain alert during screening. Removing duplicates is an important earlier step, but it does not guarantee that every duplicate has been removed.”

---

# 7. What Should Maya Do With Records That Look Like Duplicates?

Maya compares the two records.

She does not rely on the title alone.

She checks other information, such as:

* Authors
* Publication year
* Journal or source
* DOI
* Volume and issue
* Pages
* Other publication identifiers

For example:

| Field   | Record 1                                                  | Record 2                                                  |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Title   | The Effect of Artificial Intelligence on Student Learning | THE EFFECT OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE ON STUDENT LEARNING |
| Authors | Smith et al.                                              | Smith et al.                                              |
| Year    | 2025                                                      | 2025                                                      |
| Journal | Journal of Educational Technology                         | Journal of Educational Technology                         |
| DOI     | Same                                                      | Same                                                      |

The records appear to represent the same publication.

Maya therefore treats them as a duplicate rather than screening them as two independent studies.

> **Mentor:** “A title that looks the same is a signal to investigate—not necessarily proof of duplication.”

---

# 8. What If the Titles Are Similar but Not Identical?

Maya encounters another situation.

**Record 1**

```text
The Effects of Generative AI on Student Learning
```

**Record 2**

```text
Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence on University Student Learning
```

The titles are similar, but Maya cannot assume they are duplicates.

She checks the bibliographic information.

They may be:

* The same publication with different metadata
* A conference paper and a journal article
* Two different publications
* A study report and a later publication

> **Mentor:** “Never remove a record just because the titles look similar. Verify the bibliographic information first.”

---

# 9. Use Rayyan to Record Screening Decisions

Maya now begins making her screening decisions in Rayyan.

She can organize records according to her screening decisions and use labels or notes to help manage the process.

For example:

```text
Potentially eligible
Clearly exclude
Population mismatch
Wrong intervention
Wrong outcome
Wrong study design
Wrong publication type
Language issue
Other
```

The exact labels should match Maya's review needs and protocol.

> **Mentor:** “Your labels should help you understand why records were excluded or retained. Keep the screening process consistent.”

---

# 10. When Should Maya Exclude a Record?

Maya asks:

> **Maya:** “Should I exclude a study whenever I see something that looks wrong?”

> **Mentor:** “Only when you have enough information to determine that it does not meet your eligibility criteria.”

For example:

**Title:**

> *Artificial Intelligence in Corporate Employee Recruitment*

Maya's review is about AI-powered learning technologies for higher education students.

The record clearly concerns recruitment rather than learning.

She can exclude it based on the title.

But consider another record:

**Title:**

> *Artificial Intelligence and Student Learning*

There is no abstract.

Maya cannot determine the population, intervention, or study design.

She keeps the record for full-text screening.

### Simple rule

> **Clearly ineligible → Exclude**

> **Potentially eligible → Keep**

> **Insufficient information → Keep for full-text screening**

---

# 11. What If Maya Is Unsure?

Sometimes Maya cannot make a confident decision.

> **Maya:** “I'm not sure whether this study meets the criteria.”

> **Mentor:** “That's okay. Don't force a decision when the information isn't sufficient.”

Maya keeps the record for further assessment.

This reduces the risk of accidentally excluding a relevant study too early.

---

# 12. Document the Screening Process

Maya keeps track of the screening process.

At minimum, she should be able to determine:

* How many records entered title and abstract screening
* How many records were excluded
* How many records were retained for full-text screening
* Why records were excluded when an exclusion reason is recorded
* Any unusual screening issues that affected the process

For example:

```text
Records entering title/abstract screening: 3,704

Records excluded:                         2,850

Records retained for full-text screening:   854
```

These numbers will later contribute to the study-selection flow reported in the systematic review.

---

# 13. Maya's Title and Abstract Screening Workflow

Maya summarizes the process:

```text
Clean Records
      ↓
Import Into Rayyan
      ↓
Review Eligibility Criteria
      ↓
Read Title
      ↓
Read Abstract When Available
      ↓
Check Population
      ↓
Check Intervention
      ↓
Check Outcomes
      ↓
Check Study Design
      ↓
Make Screening Decision
      ↓
 ┌───────────────┬─────────────────┐
 ↓               ↓                 ↓
Clearly        Potentially      Not Enough
Ineligible     Eligible         Information
 ↓               ↓                 ↓
Exclude         Keep            Keep for
                                Full Text
                                      ↓
                         Full-Text Screening
```

During screening, Maya remembers:

```text
Different language
        ↓
Check protocol
        ↓
Follow predefined language criteria
```

and:

```text
No abstract
        ↓
Do not automatically exclude
        ↓
Use available information
        ↓
If uncertain → Full-text screening
```

and:

```text
Same-looking title
        ↓
Check authors + year + DOI + source
        ↓
Confirm whether it is actually a duplicate
```

---

# ⭐ Important Principle

> **Title and abstract screening should follow the eligibility criteria established in the protocol. Use Rayyan to organize the screening process and record decisions. Do not automatically exclude records because the abstract is missing or because the record is written in another language; first apply the protocol's language and eligibility criteria. If information is insufficient to determine eligibility, retain the record for full-text screening. If two records have identical or very similar titles, investigate their bibliographic information before deciding whether they are duplicates.**

---

# 🚀 Maya's Journey Continues

Maya finishes screening the titles and abstracts.

She now has a smaller group of records that may meet her eligibility criteria.

> **Maya:** “I have excluded the records that clearly don't fit, but I kept the ones where I wasn't sure.”

> **Mentor:** “Good. Now we need to obtain and examine the full texts.”

> **Maya:** “And that's where we make the final eligibility decision?”

> **Mentor:** “Exactly.”

Maya prepares her list of potentially eligible studies.

> **Next step: Full-Text Screening.**
